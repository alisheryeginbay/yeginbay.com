A model trained to predict the legal moves in Connect 4 uses a linear representation of each column's height; intervening on them causes models to change their choices. [Repository](https://github.com/alisheryeginbay/connect-4).

## Motivation

A while ago I read the [Othello GPT paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382) [@li2022] and decided to reproduce it. The paper trains a model whose task is to predict legal moves in the game of Othello. They further probe its activations to see whether it has an internal linear (?) representation of so called *world models*---in this case, the state of the board. Their results show that the model indeed appears to track the state of the board and uses it in its computation in a way that affects accuracy.

Instead of conducting the experiment on Othello, I decided to play around with [Connect 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connect_Four). This choice was pretty arbitrary; I wanted to reproduce it on a game that is neither complex nor very primitive.

## Setup

The game is standard Connect 4: 6×7 board; discs drop to the lowest empty cell; player who first gets 4 discs in a row (vertically, horizontally, diagonally) wins.

### Data

The training dataset comprises uniformly random legal moves. There are 5,000,000 games, generated as 50 shards of 100,000 (seeds 0–49). The first 10,000 games are held out for validation and the rest (4,990,000) games are used for training. Games have 21.3 moves on average; 8 at minimum.

Initially I generate 100,000 games for the probing dataset. I further prune the games that were present in the training dataset. 2.4% of them were pruned, so this leaves us with 97,600 games.

Each game is a sequence with length 44 and vocabulary of 10 tokens. The vocabulary consists of columns 0–6, BOS, END, and PAD. The sequence is written as BOS c1 c2 … PAD PAD. PAD tokens are ignored in the loss function.

### Model

A standard GPT-style decoder with 8 layers, 8 heads, and 512-dimensional hidden space. The total size is 25.3M parameters, which is on par with the Othello-GPT's parameters. Trained on a single NVIDIA L40S on [Modal](https://modal.com). The model's top-predicted moves are legal in 100% of the validation games. 

## Probing

I'm probing for column heights. Why not the state of the board as in the Othello paper? Because the model does not need to develop a sophisticated world model for Connect 4! When I started this experiment, Claude gave me a subtle observation---well, the model only needs to know whether a certain column is full and in order to do so the easiest thing is just to keep track of the tokens. For instance, if the model sees a sequence BOS 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 …, it is clear at the position 7 that the first column is full. 

The probes are trained on 5,000 games which I split 80/20 by games, i.e 4,000 and 1,000 for training and validation, respectively. Training split has 90,525 training positions; validation split has 22,918 positions. I trained 9 probes on embeddings and activations after each of the blocks. Activations are standardized per layer using training set's mean and standard deviation.

In order to check it causally, I've intervened at the BOS position by steering the direction of the target column towards the full height.

Steering this direction drops the probability of the target column being selected towards zero.
